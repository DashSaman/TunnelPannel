# Shared customer traffic allowance

The owner requests a monthly allowance shared by every route of a customer, administrator inspection/allocation, two-node acceptance, website/repository publication and untouched existing tunnels. The owner clarified that allowance review and grants belong entirely to the administrator. This extends the accepted per-route payload accounting, not payment collection.

Each customer workspace is one billing pool, shared by all its login accounts and source nodes. The administrator opens a dated period (30 days by default), sets an absolute byte ceiling, can increase/change it with revision checking and can open a later period after outstanding credits are safely closed. No automatic payment or historical counter reset. Expired periods deny payload until the administrator grants access. Upload plus download accepted for forwarding is the unit; probes, SOCKS negotiation and ISP framing are excluded.

The execution controller owns a durable SQLite credit ledger in the existing private registry. A gateway receives a unique hashed, route-scoped credential over the existing pinned SSH deployment. Its HTTPS callback uses a configured, validated URL, normal TLS verification, no environment proxies or redirects, bounded bodies/timeouts. It cannot choose a workspace or another route. The website only proxies requests; no database/accounting process moves there.

Credits are reserved atomically before use. Committed usage plus outstanding reserved credit never exceeds the period ceiling across concurrent nodes. Requests and cumulative reports are idempotent. Each worker durably debits its route and credit in one local transaction before forwarding. Short renewable leases fail closed on controller/network outage. Monotonic deadlines subtract request elapsed time; restart requires online reconciliation. A worker first fences unused credit locally before returning it. Expired or unreachable reservations are never optimistically refunded. UI separates reported usage, held credit and allocatable balance; stale/offline held credit may temporarily reduce availability, never multiply allowance.

The gateway returns idle credit promptly. Crash recovery replays an outstanding request before fencing/settling it. Graceful stop/reconciliation first proves its worker is stopped, reads the preserved private usage ledger and retires the route credential transactionally; only then can unused credit be returned. Missing/corrupt accounting fails closed and is reported. Records survive route deletion, period renewal and reinstall.

Enabling a pool must reject existing active unmetered/historical routes; no silent partial coverage and no automatic migration/restart. New routes inherit an enabled pool. A serialization lease coordinates first enable with route admission. The original two live groups and ten members remain unchanged; tests use separate temporary routes/workspace. Existing per-route speed, quota and connection limits remain independent lower bounds.

Acceptance includes concurrent opposite-source routes against one allowance, exact exhaustion, idempotent lost-response replay, process/API restart, unused credit return, admin top-up/new period/history and customer isolation. Fresh CI, drain/baselines, per-installation encrypted resident backups with separate keys, reviewed helper hashes and immutable tree/expected-head publication remain mandatory. No main merge, heartbeat resume, domain/payment/SSO changes or unrelated website edits.
